# Sourcing-first video production standard

Status: standing production requirement
Budget: $0
Default: research and assemble existing media before generating anything

## Editorial goal
Make a researched, entertaining video that feels edited—not a slideshow. Start with a specific viewer promise, build a story, and use visual evidence to support every beat. Automation should save time, not lower the quality bar.

## Visual sourcing hierarchy — strict priority
1. **Social-platform creator content (primary/main footage).** Search relevant public posts, creator videos, interviews, demonstrations, eyewitness clips and specialist explainers on suitable social platforms. Prefer the most informative, distinctive, high-value material that directly serves the story. Record the exact post, creator, timestamps and what the clip contributes. Public visibility is not permission to download, edit, re-upload, or commercially reuse content. Use creator permission, an applicable licence, a platform-native reuse/remix feature whose terms cover the intended use, or a reviewed legal basis. Follow platform terms; do not bypass access controls or mass-download creator content. If clearance is missing, mark it HOLD/PERMISSION_NEEDED and find a lawful alternative.
2. **Free stock, public-domain and openly licensed media (supplementary).** Use for establishing shots, context, transitions, cutaways and evidence not covered by cleared social content. Verify the individual asset's licence, commercial-use/adaptation terms, attribution and restrictions.
3. **Original graphics and diagrams (supporting).** Use maps, timelines, labels, comparisons and data visuals to explain the story.
4. **AI-generated video (gap-filler only; lowest priority).** Use sparingly when real cleared footage cannot cover a specific beat or when an explicitly illustrative visual materially improves comprehension. Never use AI just to pad runtime or replace good source footage. Do not spend limited credits before the edit plan is settled.

If social content cannot be cleared, do not silently replace it with unlicensed material. Search for another cleared creator asset, use a permitted platform-native embed/link or commentary format where appropriate, or revise the story.

## Required media inventory
For each candidate asset record:
- exact source URL, title, creator and access date
- platform and exact post/video ID where applicable
- planned scene and the point it proves
- exact timestamps for proposed clips
- licence/permission evidence and required attribution
- any embedded music, logos or third-party content
- retrieval method and platform-term checks
- status: APPROVED, PERMISSION_NEEDED, REPLACE or HOLD_FOR_REVIEW

A public URL, a short excerpt, or credit alone is not a reuse licence. A candidate can be used for research without being cleared for publication. Do not bypass access controls or mass-download creator content.

## Storyboard before edit
1. Hook: a concrete question, surprise or tension in the first 1–2 seconds.
2. Context: why this matters to this viewer.
3. Three to five evidence-led beats, each with a distinct visual.
4. Payoff: the conclusion follows from the evidence.
5. Close: one specific question that invites a meaningful comment.

Separate confirmed facts, credible reporting, interpretation and speculation. Do not present rumours as confirmed facts.

## Assembly, narration and credits
- Main visual substance should come from cleared social-platform creator content wherever relevant and available. Free stock is supplementary; AI footage fills specific gaps only.
- Use short, purposeful cuts and varied shot scale. Avoid holding static title cards for most of the video.
- Narration must sound conversational, expressive and paced to the edit—not like a robotic readout. Test free voices and delivery settings; use natural pauses and sentence-level timing. If no acceptable free voice is available, do not call the draft finished.
- Captions must be accurate, readable on a phone, and timed to the spoken words.
- Music and sound effects must have a verified licence and sit below narration.
- End the film with a deliberate, readable **movie-style end-credits sequence** listing used visual sources/creators and required credits, followed by any production credit or closing card. Keep the full detailed source/licence ledger and any required description credits as well.
- **Licence terms take precedence over design preference:** if a licence requires attribution in a particular place or form, satisfy that requirement even if it means an additional on-screen credit.
- Do not assume a platform post's soundtrack, logos, people or embedded third-party media are cleared just because the creator uploaded it.

## Mandatory pre-publish quality gate
Do not upload automatically just because rendering succeeded. Render a review artifact and check:
- visual relevance and variety; no blank/placeholder scenes
- first 2 seconds establish a strong promise
- narration pronunciation, pace, emotion, volume and intelligibility
- captions, spelling, factual accuracy and transitions
- every third-party asset has a defensible reuse basis
- end credits are legible and complete, with item-specific requirements honoured
- title and thumbnail are accurate, compelling and not misleading
- final runtime and audio/video sync
- human approval recorded before public upload

If the gate fails, produce a draft artifact only; never auto-publish a low-quality fallback. Keep the proven upload step separate from the creative pipeline.
