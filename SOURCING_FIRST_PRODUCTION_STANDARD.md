# Sourcing-first video production standard

Status: production requirement for the next Zelda remake video
Budget: $0
Default: research and assemble existing media before generating anything

## Editorial goal
Make a researched, entertaining video that feels edited—not a slideshow. Start with a specific viewer promise, build a story, and use visual evidence to support every beat. Automation should save time, not lower the quality bar.

## Asset sourcing order
1. Official Nintendo trailers, gameplay showcases, press pages and announcements: discover and link them first. Treat them as copyrighted source material, not automatically reusable footage.
2. Existing creator footage, reviews, interviews and analysis: identify exact moments that support a specific claim. Prefer permission, creator-provided press/media assets, platform-native remix features, or a reviewed applicable copyright exception. Never assume a short duration or attribution makes reuse legal.
3. Reusable libraries: Wikimedia Commons, Internet Archive, Pexels and other free catalogues where the specific item licence permits the intended use. Verify the item page, source, licence, commercial-use/adaptation terms, attribution, and any restrictions.
4. Original supporting visuals: custom diagrams, timelines, maps, comparison cards and typography. Use these to explain—not to replace every real visual.
5. AI-generated assets only for gaps that materially improve the story. Do not spend limited credits before the script and shot list are approved.

## Required media inventory
For each candidate asset record:
- exact source URL, title, creator and access date
- planned scene and the point it proves
- exact timestamps for proposed clips
- licence/permission evidence and required attribution
- any embedded music, logos or third-party content
- retrieval method and platform-term checks
- status: APPROVED, PERMISSION_NEEDED, REPLACE or HOLD_FOR_REVIEW

A public YouTube URL is not a reuse licence. A candidate can be used for research without being cleared for publication. If rights are unclear, request permission, use a native clipping/remix feature whose terms cover the use, or replace it with original commentary and permitted visuals. Do not bypass access controls or mass-download creator content.

## Storyboard before edit
1. Hook: a concrete question, surprise or tension in the first 1–2 seconds.
2. Context: why this matters to this viewer.
3. Three to five evidence-led beats, each with a distinct visual.
4. Payoff: the conclusion follows from the evidence.
5. Close: one specific question that invites a meaningful comment.

For an Ocarina of Time remake topic, do not imply Nintendo has confirmed a remake or specific changes unless verified by a primary source. Separate confirmed facts, credible reporting, and speculation. If no remake is officially confirmed, frame the video honestly as a wishlist/analysis: “5 changes an Ocarina of Time remake would need,” not “5 confirmed changes.”

## Assembly and audio
- Prioritise relevant existing footage/images; use stock only for supporting context, not as random filler.
- Use short, purposeful cuts and varied shot scale. Avoid holding static title cards for most of the video.
- Narration must sound natural and be paced to the edit. Captions must be accurate and readable on a phone.
- Music and sound effects must have a verified licence and sit below narration.
- Avoid copyrighted game music unless permission/rights are established.
- Keep source credits and licence evidence in the project folder and description where required.

## Mandatory pre-publish quality gate
Do not upload automatically just because rendering succeeded. Render a review artifact and check:
- visual relevance and variety; no blank/placeholder scenes
- first 2 seconds establish a strong promise
- narration pronunciation, pace, volume and intelligibility
- captions, spelling, factual accuracy and transitions
- every third-party asset has a defensible reuse basis
- title and thumbnail are accurate, compelling and not misleading
- final runtime and audio/video sync
- human approval recorded before public upload

If the gate fails, produce a draft artifact only; never auto-publish a low-quality fallback. Keep the proven upload step separate from the creative pipeline.
