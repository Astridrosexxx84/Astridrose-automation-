# Video 001 — Source-First Production Brief

Status: topic selected for media audit; no video rendered or uploaded
Budget: $0
Primary goal: prove we can make one genuinely engaging, polished video from free, reusable media before adding trend detection.

## Topic
**Earth From Space: 5 Things That Look Completely Different From Orbit**

Why this is the first candidate:
- Broad visual variety: night lights, storms, auroras, coastlines, deserts, clouds, glaciers, Earth observation and astronaut photography.
- NASA provides a large collection of science and space imagery/video, much of it usable under its media guidelines; every item still needs a source/rights check.
- Wikimedia Commons can supply additional openly licensed media where the exact file licence is recorded.
- We can tell a coherent visual story with original narration and maps/labels rather than stitch unrelated clips together.
- Topic does not depend on trends or copyrighted entertainment footage.

## Important factual and rights rule
NASA-produced content is generally not copyrighted in the United States, but exceptions exist for third-party material, logos, identifiable people and endorsement implications. Check the individual asset and NASA's current media guidelines:
https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media/
NASA Image and Video Library:
https://images.nasa.gov/
Wikimedia Commons free video resources:
https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Commons:Free_media_resources/Video/en
For each Wikimedia asset, check the actual file page and licence, attribution and adaptation terms. Do not treat a search result or host website as blanket permission.
The video must add original educational narration, story, context, sequencing and analysis; not be a raw compilation.

## Story premise
A cinematic, evidence-led journey that helps viewers notice things they might never see from the ground. Every shot must support a specific observation. Avoid generic “beautiful planet” filler.

## Proposed structure (3–4 minutes)
1. Hook (0:00–0:12): “From space, Earth looks familiar—until you notice what the planet is actually showing you.”
2. Night lights (0:12–0:45): population and infrastructure patterns; explain that lights show human activity but not the full story of where people live.
3. Weather systems (0:45–1:20): cloud bands, cyclones and storm structures; explain what satellite views reveal.
4. Thin atmosphere / limb (1:20–1:55): why the visible atmospheric layer is visually thin and why it matters; use a sourced, carefully worded explanation.
5. Natural colour and false colour (1:55–2:35): explain that satellite imagery may use enhanced or false colour to reveal data, not always show what the human eye would see.
6. Moving ice, rivers and coastlines (2:35–3:10): show change over time using dated, comparable imagery if suitable rights-cleared assets are found.
7. Payoff and comment question (3:10–3:25): “Which view changed how you picture Earth most—and what should we explore next?”

## Media-first gate — must pass before scripting is locked
Build an inventory with at least:
- 12 usable, high-quality video clips from at least 4 distinct visual categories
- 8 high-resolution stills/maps/visualizations that add context
- exact asset URL, creator/agency, title, date, rights/usage note, attribution, planned scene and status for each item
- no copyrighted soundtrack by default; narration-first mix and verified free music only if it improves the edit
- confirm clips are accessible in a lawful download/production route and can be edited without a paid subscription

If we cannot find sufficient varied, high-quality clips, change the subject before producing. Do not fill gaps with random stock or AI-generated filler.

## Quality gate
- Start with motion and a compelling visual in the first two seconds.
- Each section makes one clear point, supported by the shot.
- Mix wide views, close details, animations/maps and short on-screen labels.
- Original narration with human-readable pacing; accurate captions.
- Use licensed/public-domain material only after item-level review; keep source credits and rights ledger.
- No slideshow with robotic narration; no static title cards held to fill time.
- Render a draft first, inspect the actual video and audio, fix weaknesses, and only then ask for final approval to publish.
- Never upload a failed quality test or an unreviewed draft.

## Packaging draft
Title: Earth From Space: 5 Things You Never Notice From the Ground
Thumbnail: one striking, rights-cleared Earth image with simple, readable text: “EARTH, REVEALED”
Description should credit each used asset according to its terms and distinguish genuine-colour photography from scientific false-colour imagery.
