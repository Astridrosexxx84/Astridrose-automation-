# Video 001 — Source-First Production Brief

Status: first rendered candidate approved as a technical proof of concept; quality revision required
Budget: $0
Primary goal: turn the first render into a genuinely engaging, polished video using the production hierarchy below.

## Topic
**Earth From Space: 5 Things You Never Notice From the Ground**

## Mandatory visual sourcing order
1. **Social-platform creator content first:** search for relevant public posts, original creator footage, specialist explainers and interviews that add real information or a distinct perspective. Record exact URLs, creators, clip timestamps, rights/permission evidence and platform terms. Do not download/re-upload or edit creator material unless permission, a suitable licence, a valid legal basis, or platform-native reuse terms allow the intended use. A public post or attribution alone is not permission.
2. **Free stock/public-domain/openly licensed footage second:** use as supporting cutaways, context, transitions and to cover specific evidence gaps. Check every asset's own terms and credit requirements.
3. **AI-generated footage last:** only to illustrate a specific gap that cannot be covered by cleared real footage. Do not use it as filler and do not spend paid credits; budget remains $0.

If the social-first sourcing pass cannot find enough legally usable material, expand the search, ask creators for permission, or revise the edit/story. Never quietly treat an uncleared viral clip as reusable.

## Revision requirements based on first candidate
- Replace the current robotic-sounding narration with the most natural free voice/delivery that can be run reliably. Try alternatives and delivery controls; assess the complete rendered video.
- Rebuild the edit around the actual strongest cleared footage, not around the existing NASA-only selection. NASA clips remain eligible supporting footage after individual checks.
- Put a deliberate, movie-style credit roll at the end of the video. List used footage sources/creators and required credits in a readable end sequence. Retain the full credits/licence ledger and description credits. Honour any licence that specifically requires attribution elsewhere.
- Maintain original educational narration and story; do not make a raw compilation.
- Keep captions aligned to the final narration and footage.
- Continue to render a complete review video only; no upload without explicit approval.

## Story premise
A cinematic, evidence-led journey that helps viewers notice things they might never see from the ground. Every shot must support a specific observation. Avoid generic “beautiful planet” filler.

## Proposed structure (3–4 minutes)
1. Hook (0:00–0:12): “From space, Earth looks familiar—until you notice what the planet is actually showing you.”
2. Night lights: population and infrastructure patterns; explain limits.
3. Weather systems: cloud bands, storms and satellite observations.
4. Thin atmosphere / limb: careful, sourced explanation.
5. Natural colour and false colour: distinguish human-visible colour from processed data.
6. Moving ice, rivers and coastlines: show change over time using comparable, rights-cleared imagery.
7. Payoff and comment question: ask which view changed how the viewer pictures Earth most.
8. **End credits:** movie-style source roll with required credits; enough screen time to read.

## Quality gate
- Strong moving image in the first two seconds.
- Each section makes one clear point supported by the footage.
- Use cleared social footage as the main substance when relevant; stock supports; AI fills gaps.
- Conversational narration, accurate captions and intentional sound mix.
- Credits at the end, with any additional attribution required by licence.
- No slideshow with robotic narration; no static title cards held to fill time.
- Inspect the complete rendered video and revise before asking for a final yay/nay.
- Never upload a failed quality test or an unreviewed draft.

## Packaging draft
Title: Earth From Space: 5 Things You Never Notice From the Ground
Thumbnail: one striking, cleared Earth image with simple readable text: “EARTH, REVEALED”
Description: credit each used asset according to its terms and distinguish genuine-colour photography from scientific false-colour imagery.
